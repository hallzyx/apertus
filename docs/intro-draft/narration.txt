A claim can sound convincing. But does the official voting booklet actually support it? That is the question behind Apertus Evidence Lab.

The OST challenge asks us to compare a claim with a Swiss voting booklet: supported, contradicted, or not established. And the claim and document may be in different languages.

Our starting point is a Jev-style approach: keep the language model frozen, and train a small decision head on its internal representations. Here, Apertus version one point five supplies those representations.

We build the document pipeline around it. Extract page text, combine keyword and multilingual semantic retrieval, and pack original passages into an eight-thousand-token context budget. Production needs only the booklet and the claim.

We separate training and development validation by connected voting events. We compare context budgets and test what happens with empty or wrong-event documents. Performance drops support document dependence, with limitations.

On two hundred seventy-six development examples, the best base configuration scores point six seven two Macro F-one. The original full-context head reaches point eight nine four. Our selected hybrid eight-K system reaches point nine four nine, and point nine four two across languages.

That selected configuration uses about forty-three percent fewer prompt tokens than the original full-context system. These are development results, not the organizer's hidden evaluation.

The interface returns a decision with inspectable source passages, page numbers, and downloadable results. Those passages show the source context; they are not claimed as a validated minimal proof.
